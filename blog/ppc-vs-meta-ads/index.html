PPC vs Meta Ads: Differences & When to Use Each

Search captures demand that already exists; Meta creates demand before the search. Most growing brands eventually need both.

By REVOLVYN · 15 September 2026 · 3 min read

PPC and Meta advertising are often discussed as if they are competing versions of the same thing.

They are not.

The biggest difference is usually how the audience arrives at the advertising experience.

Search advertising can meet someone who is already expressing intent. Meta advertising can create demand, introduce a product and build interest before the person searches for it.

For many brands, the two can work together.

Search starts with a query

When someone searches for a product, service or problem, the query itself gives the advertiser information about intent.

A person searching for a specific service may already know what they want.

That makes search useful for demand that already exists.

But search also has a limitation: you generally need people to search for the category, problem or brand in the first place.

Meta can create attention earlier

Meta ads can reach people before they actively search.

That makes creative particularly important.

The ad has to earn attention and communicate relevance without waiting for the user to type a query.

For a visual consumer brand, that can be a major advantage. Demonstrations, creator videos, product visuals and strong hooks can introduce a product to people who were not actively looking for it.

So which one should a brand choose?

The answer depends on the demand structure.

Ask:

Do people already search for the thing I sell?

If yes, search can capture existing demand.

Then ask:

Can my product benefit from visual discovery, creator content or problem-led advertising?

If yes, Meta can help create and develop demand.

Many businesses can benefit from both.

The creative requirement is different

Search ads need strong alignment between the query, message and landing page.

Meta creative has a larger attention burden because the user was not necessarily looking for an advertisement.

That means Meta campaigns often need a larger creative testing engine.

A simple way to think about the channels

Google/PPC: “I am looking for something.”

ong>Meta: “I did not plan to look for this, but this is relevant to me.”

Real customer journeys are less neat than those sentences, but the distinction is useful when planning a media mix.

Don't judge channels in isolation

A customer might discover a brand through a creator video, visit the website later through search and return through retargeting.

If you judge every channel only by its final click, you can miss how the journey actually works.

That is why full-funnel planning matters.

The right question is not “Meta or Google?”

It is:

Which combination of channels helps the brand create demand, capture demand and convert it efficiently?
